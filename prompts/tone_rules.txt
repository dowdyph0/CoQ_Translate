TONE — Caves of Qud has a uniquely ornate, literary prose style. Reproduce it faithfully:
- Register: extremely elevated, baroque, sometimes archaic. Inspired by Gene Wolfe's "Book of the New Sun" (far-future quasi-medieval vocabulary), Jack Vance's Dying Earth (florid wit and irony), Frank Herbert's Dune (desert mythology, invented terminology), and Middle English poetry (Chaucer register for in-world historical texts).
- Vocabulary: prefer rare, precise, or archaic words over plain equivalents. The game freely uses words like "gravens", "evanesced", "lathe" (as a verb), "facile", "imago", "phenomenic", "meyvn". Match this density in the target language.
- Untranslatable proper nouns and invented coinages (copy verbatim): Qud, Joppa, Barathrum, Hindren, Moghra'yi, Ekuemekiyye, Ibul, Yawningmoon, zetachrome, eigenrifle, Mechanimists, Glimmer, and any other invented proper name or neologism that has no natural equivalent in the target language.
- Fixed canonical translations — always use these exact equivalents: "dromad" → "dromada"; "the Spindle" / "Spindle" → "la Aguja" / "Aguja"; "True Kin" → "Verdadera Estirpe"; "the Eaters" → "los Devoradores".
- Translatable descriptive names: render these with their meaning into the target language — "snapjaw", "watervine", "glowpad", "salve injector", faction names with clear English meaning, creature names made of common English words, item descriptions. Use judgment: if the English is clearly a compound of known words, translate it; if it sounds invented and opaque, keep it.
- Titles that exist in both languages (sultan, caliph, etc.): keep them as-is since they are already cross-lingual cultural words.
- Greeting/salutation "Live and drink" is the world's canonical phrase — adapt it with equal ritual weight, do not paraphrase it casually.
- Tone spectrum: item descriptions are poetic and evocative ("a large piece of rock, older than every idea"); NPC dialogue is intimate yet formal; tutorial text is direct but never flat. Match each register precisely.
- NEVER simplify, modernize, or flatten the prose. If the English is unusual, the translation must be equally unusual in the target language.
